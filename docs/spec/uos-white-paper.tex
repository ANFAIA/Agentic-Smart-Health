\noindent{\normalsize\bfseries Executive summary}
\vspace{0.15cm}

{\small

\noindent\textbf{The problem.} Dental acquisitions often arrive as separate scans,
CBCT series, photographs and reports. Their spatial, temporal and semantic relations may
remain in individual applications or require further estimation. UOS proposes an explicit,
portable description of those relations and of how each result was produced.

\noindent\textbf{What we built.} UOS (Unified Oral Scene) is an open composition container.
The reference writer leaves acquired originals outside the ZIP and identifies them by
hash; it carries converted geometry, derived representations, metadata and views. A
specification, JSON Schema, validator and synthetic conformance fixtures accompany the
implementation. Access to referenced originals still requires an external repository.

\noindent\textbf{What has been measured.} On public Teeth3DS+ meshes, regenerating the
mesh through the pipeline differs from its input by at most $4.59\times10^{-6}$\,mm, a
numerical-preservation result rather than scanner accuracy. In case~A, a source audit found
that the historical 0.666\,mm registration residual was computed on two DICOM series mixed
together; registered separately, neither series reaches the overlap the review gate requires.
In case~B, the production registration's pose varies by 1--3\,mm with its random seed and
can flip by about $180^\circ$ at an unchanged residual. No landmark-based registration error
exists for either case. Experimental tooth labels, photograph-based regional colour and
reconstructed roots remain prototypes: in case~A the default crown--gingiva margin lies a
median 3.5\,mm from a periodontist's blind line, and reconstructed roots in case~B reach
40\,mm.

\noindent\textbf{What we are not claiming.} UOS is a proposal with one implementation, not
a standard. Validation of a container does not establish clinical correctness. The figures
come from two clinical cases, three CBCT volumes of a licensed cohort and the public
Teeth3DS+ corpus; none of this is a clinical study. Results that did not reach their
pre-specified criteria are reported with the same weight as those that did.

\vspace{0.25cm}
\noindent\emph{Keywords:} dental informatics, interoperability, digital twin, provenance,
CBCT, intraoral scanning, DICOM, glTF, FHIR, 3D Gaussian splatting
}

\section{A case that arrives in pieces}\label{sec:problem}

\subsection{What a practice holds after one visit}

Consider what a dental practice actually holds after a single patient visit that involved
imaging. There is one or two STL files from the intraoral scanner, a directory of several
hundred DICOM slices from the cone-beam CT, a handful of clinical photographs in whatever
the camera wrote, and a PDF report. Four artefacts, four programs, four coordinate
systems, and no machine-readable statement of how any of them relate to any other.

Some relations are known at acquisition, while others, such as cross-modal registration,
must be estimated later. They may remain in a person's memory or inside a vendor's
application. When the case changes hands, recipients need those relations together with
an account of their origin and limitations. Reconstructing them repeatedly costs time and
can produce inconsistent results.

\subsection{What it costs, and who pays it}\label{sec:cost}

The cost of fragmentation falls in four places.

\paragraph{The second reader.} The clinician who acquired the case has the relations in
their head and does not need them written down, which is exactly why they never get
written down. The cost lands on the second reader. A colleague opening the same case a week
later sees a grey arch in one program and a stack of slices in another, and must decide
whether a finding described in prose belongs to the crown they are looking at. In practice
they re-derive what they can and take the rest on trust.

\paragraph{The laboratory.} What reaches a dental laboratory is usually an STL and a
written instruction. The STL is geometry and nothing else: no colour, no units declared, no
statement of which tooth is which, no indication of what was measured versus what the
scanner interpolated across a gap. Everything the clinic knew that is not geometry has to
be re-expressed in prose, and prose does not survive a workflow with three handoffs.


\paragraph{The next visit.} Comparing visits requires a suitable anatomical reference,
registration and landmark localisation. Recording those steps makes a comparison auditable;
sharing a coordinate frame alone does not establish whether a change is biological or error.

\paragraph{The archive.} An open manifest can preserve the interpretation of a case beyond
one application. Its usefulness still depends on preserving its payloads, including external
originals, and maintaining the information needed to retrieve and interpret them.

\subsection{Why choosing a different format does not fix it}


Existing standards already cover substantial parts of this problem. The design question is
how to assemble them into a documented dental workflow, and what additional conventions
that workflow needs.

\begin{longtable}{L{3.0cm} L{12.2cm}}
\toprule
\textbf{Format} & \textbf{Role in the proposed composition}\\
\midrule
\endhead
\textbf{DICOM}~\cite{dicom} & Defines images, spatial registrations, segmentations,
photographs, encapsulated models and reports. DICOMweb supplies web transport. UOS must
justify its additional scene and provenance conventions alongside those existing objects,
rather than claim that DICOM cannot relate the acquisitions.\\
\textbf{glTF}~\cite{gltf} & Supplies the scene representation and extension mechanism.
The UOS manifest supplies case-level conventions for external acquisitions, provenance

\textbf{FHIR}~\cite{fhir} & Supplies resources for clinical exchange and references to media.
A geometry attachment does not by itself define a dental spatial-composition profile;
connectors must declare their FHIR version and mapping.\\
\bottomrule
\end{longtable}

UOS proposes a particular composition contract across these representations.
\S\ref{sec:related} distinguishes intended mappings from implemented connectors.

\subsection{Motivation}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Cases combine modalities.} Implant and orthodontic planning, among other
  workflows, can involve both a CBCT and an intraoral scan of the same patient, and the
  relation between them then has to be established for each case. We report no adoption
  figures.

  \item \textbf{One representation can hold surfaces and volumes.} 3D Gaussian splatting
  \cite{kerbl2023} and its attenuation variants \cite{xgaussian,r2gaussian} give a common
  primitive for data that was previously either a mesh or a voxel grid. Whether that helps is
  an empirical question; \S\ref{sec:gs} reports what was measured.

  \item \textbf{Automated processing needs explicit provenance.} Recipients need to know
  whether a result was acquired, calculated or inferred, and whether it was reviewed.
  These distinctions support evaluation and audit; regulatory qualification depends on
  each software module's intended purpose, not solely on its use of a trained model.

\end{enumerate}

\subsection{What this document is, and what it is not}

This is not the specification. The normative document is the \emph{UOS Format Specification
v0.3}~\cite{uosspec}, which defines the container, the manifest field by field, the
conformance levels and the validation algorithm, and which is accompanied by a published
JSON Schema, a reference implementation and a conformance fixture bank (\S\ref{sec:status}).
An engineer writing a reader should work from those.

What follows describes the problem the format addresses, the design decisions behind it
and what has been measured, including results that did not reach their criteria.

\paragraph{Clinical material.} Two pseudonymised clinical cases are used, and every figure
below names the one it comes from. \textbf{Case~A}, from which the container examples are
drawn, is a maxillary arch with one intraoral scan, a CBCT exported as two series of 100 and
301 instances with irregular slice spacing, nine image files and three reports.
\textbf{Case~B} is a second patient with a dense CBCT series (578 slices at 0.30\,mm) and
several intraoral scans of both arches, including two independent acquisitions from the
same session. Neither case has landmark-based ground truth for registration.

\begin{finding}{Design rule: distinguishable provenance}
Data of different origin must not be stored identically: an automatic alignment and one
reviewed by a clinician; a colour estimated from the patient's photographs and one from a
fallback gradient; a tooth boundary drawn by a network and one drawn by a person; a DICOM
series held inside a container and one identified only by hash. Where the format can enforce
the distinction it does, and where it cannot it requires the distinction to be declared. The
residual on every registration, the verification field that defaults to unverified and the
per-value declaration of how a clinical figure was extracted all follow from this rule
\cite{uosspec}.
\end{finding}

\section{What UOS is}\label{sec:what}

\subsection{Composition, not encoding}

Our claim is that the missing artefact is a \emph{composition} format, and that composition
is a different problem from encoding. Encoding asks how to store a mesh or a volume well,
and it is largely solved. Composition asks what else must be written down so that the stored
things remain usable together: which coordinate frame each lives in, what transform relates
them and how well it fits, which visit each belongs to, which of them a model produced, and
what any of it is worth.


A \code{.uos} is an uncompressed ZIP whose first entry is \code{manifest.json}.
Version 0.3 requires \code{STORE}, and the writer and validator enforce it. Uncompressed
storage permits direct access within an entry; it is not needed merely to fetch a complete
entry with HTTP range requests, since a compressed entry can also be fetched and then
decompressed. No end-to-end range-access benchmark is reported here. The storage cost is
measured in \S\ref{sec:evidence}; a different compression policy would require a format change.

The reference writer leaves acquired originals outside the container. It records whole-file
hashes and, for DICOM series, per-file entries with optional SOP Instance UIDs and PixelData
hashes. These identify and help verify data a recipient already holds; they do not retrieve
it. Optional \code{locators} can name a DICOMweb endpoint, PACS or other repository, but
remain hints. The validator does not resolve them or guarantee future availability.

The container carries the composed scene, representations, metadata and views. Consequently,
it is not a self-contained archive of the acquisitions. Preservation requires retaining the
external originals and their access arrangements. The format also permits embedded payloads,
which the validator checks against their byte hashes and inventory. This is distinct from
the reference writer's external-original policy and does not define an archival profile.

\subsection{The three planes: measured, presented, inferred}\label{sec:planes}

The format defines three logical planes. The writer and validator enforce several
separation rules; the implementation limitations below qualify what that enforcement covers.

\begin{longtable}{L{2.6cm} L{3.2cm} L{9.4cm}}
\toprule
\textbf{Plane} & \textbf{Where} & \textbf{What it is}\\
\midrule
\endhead
\textbf{Data} & \code{assets[]} & What was acquired, and what the pipeline computed from it
without a model. Immutable: a writer never edits an asset in place.\\
\textbf{Presentation} & \code{views.json} & What the clinician was looking at --- camera,
visible layers, clipping. Disposable and cheap to rewrite.\\
\textbf{Inference} & \code{derived/} & Anything a model produced. \textbf{Removable}: the
reference implementation writes a validated successor without these assets, updating
dependent manifest entries and the chain and clearing saved views that may depend on
inferred labels.\\
\bottomrule
\end{longtable}


The contract names three processing layers: \textbf{1} acquisition or transcription,
\textbf{2} deterministic computation with declared sources, and \textbf{3} model inference.
The names are provenance categories, not medical-device classifications.

Implementation limits qualify that separation. Layer numbers do not establish
bitwise reproducibility. The optional \code{uos\_fidelity\_provenance} extension, version 1.0,
records per-asset processing, software, parameters, seeds and scoped repeatability evidence;
an unknown result remains unknown. Model-dependent appearance and segmentation-conditioned
fitted fields are separated as inference. Report ingestion distinguishes rules, OCR and LLM
provenance. Inferred regional findings travel separately under \code{derived/}, one
asset per observation, with layer 3 and model metadata. Sources are linked to supplied
reports when resolvable; otherwise an explicit unresolved status, validator warning and
human-review reason preserve the limitation. Independently computed colour stays in layer 2.

\begin{finding}{What removing inference does establish}
The validator checks that declared layer-3 assets reside under \code{derived/} and detects
several forms of label leakage into scene payloads. Removing inference also requires
updating its manifest entries and dependent references, then revalidating the result.
This supports modular packaging. It does not establish clinical correctness, prove that
all hidden inference has been detected, or grant permission to distribute or use software
in a particular jurisdiction.
\end{finding}

\subsection{Every relation carries its error and its author}\label{sec:relations}

The formal object a container stores is a labelled graph. Each asset declares exactly one
coordinate frame; each registration is an edge carrying a rigid transform between two
frames; and one frame, conventionally the intraoral scanner's, is canonical. Placing an
asset in the canonical frame means composing transforms along a path to it, which is well
defined only if such a path exists. That connectivity requirement is normative
\cite{uosspec}: an asset in an unreachable frame is not partially placed, it is one whose
position is undefined, and a viewer that draws it anyway is inventing a pose. The algebra is
in Appendix~\ref{app:math}.


The CBCT-to-scanner edges reported here were estimated by point-to-point ICP with a
closed-form (Kabsch) update, registering scanner vertices to CBCT Gaussian centres above an
intensity threshold (Appendix~\ref{app:icp}). The residual stored with the edge is the RMS
nearest-neighbour distance at the converged pose over the scanner points that lie within
1\,mm of the target. It is the minimised criterion evaluated on its own support, so it
describes the fit and is not a target registration error (TRE). In case~A it was 0.666\,mm,
computed on a CBCT extraction later found to mix two series (\S\ref{sec:endtoend}).

In case~B a pre-specified consistency measurement showed how little such a residual
constrains the pose. Changing only the random subsample seed of the production registration
moved the crowns by 1--3\,mm, and in about 30\,\% of seeds the arch converged rotated by
roughly $180^\circ$. A rotated pose gave an RMS of 0.679\,mm, within the 0.664--0.680\,mm
range of the unrotated ones; only the overlap fraction differed (3--9\,\% against
13--16\,\%, all below the 20\,\% gate). This is a precision result for one case and one
method, not an accuracy figure.

The contract supports \code{max\_error\_mm}, \code{target\_registration\_error\_mm} and
\code{tre\_region}. A written landmark protocol and a tool that computes TRE from points
marked outside the system exist, but no specialist landmarks have been collected, so no TRE
is reported for either case. The registration in case~A declares the use
\code{visualization} and is unverified; neither the residual nor that declaration certifies
suitability for a clinical task. The contract declares each transform rigid; the reference
writer emits rigid transforms, but the validator does not yet check rigidity. Covariance and
uncertainty propagation are not implemented.

\begin{finding}{Why retain the registration graph}
Keeping registrations explicit preserves their direction, method, residual and review
status. The reference resolver chooses the shortest path, with an identifier-based tie
break, and warns when alternative paths disagree. This is a deterministic convention,
not an uncertainty-optimal estimate: fewer edges need not mean greater accuracy.
The current discrepancy check compares matrix coefficients; it is not a spatial TRE or
a propagated uncertainty bound.
\end{finding}

\subsection{What is inside one, in practice}\label{sec:inside}

The reference container used throughout this document holds a real maxillary case: an
intraoral scan, a CBCT, nine image files (six intraoral photographs and three digital
radiographs) and three reports, contributed by the collaborating practice and processed
pseudonymised. The scan, the images and the reports are identified by SHA-256 and are not
stored in the ZIP. Table~\ref{tab:inside} describes that container as written in August 2026
by writer versions before 0.6.0. Its CBCT density field and its 397-slice volume were built
from an extraction that, as audited later, merged two DICOM series (\S\ref{sec:endtoend}), so
they do not describe a coherent single-series volume.

The most recent inventoried container of case~A (2 October 2026, archived appearance
restored) declares 24 assets, 13 of them external, and validates at UOS-Core with 0 errors
and 42 warnings. It carries no volume and does not yet resolve the DICOM source of its CBCT
field, which still derives from the mixed extraction. Its appearance layer is reported with
113{,}540 Gaussians, although it is declared bit-identical to the August artefact for which
the table gives 113{,}827; the discrepancy is unresolved. The source-separated runs of
7 October 2026 were not inventoried at this level of detail.

Two properties of the table matter beyond the numbers. \textbf{None of the three extensions is
required}: everything we add adds information, and a conforming reader that understands none
of them must still be able to open the case --- otherwise an open format is one only its
issuer can read in full. And the container is \textbf{logically append-only}: modifying a
case is not editing it, it is writing a new version of the manifest that names the previous
one's hash. The chain records internal version consistency. Without a trusted external anchor or
verified signatures, it does not establish authorship or prevent an attacker from rewriting
the chain and recomputing its hashes.

\subsection{How a new modality enters}\label{sec:newmodality}

Dentistry is not only surfaces and densities. Shade is colour under a stated illuminant;
periodontal status is a set of per-site measurements over time; and there is a growing body
of work on the oral microbiome, where the datum is neither a mesh nor a volume but a
per-site quantity attached to anatomy. A container that can only hold triangles and voxels
forces every such datum into a PDF, which is where clinical information goes to become
unqueryable.

``Extensible'' is easy to claim, so it deserves a concrete answer. Suppose a study wants to
attach microbial load per periodontal site to a case. None of it is geometry. Attaching it
takes four declarations and no change to the format; defining its clinical semantics is a
separate task that the format does not perform:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item An \textbf{asset} carrying the measurements, with its own hash, media type and the
  visit it belongs to.
  \item A \textbf{frame}, here the canonical one since sites are named anatomically
  rather than positioned, so that the connectivity rule is satisfied.
  \item An \textbf{extension} entry declaring the schema of what was added, listed as
  \emph{used} so that a reader without it still opens the case and can say
  what it left unread.
  \item A \textbf{descriptor} stating, per column, the unit, whether the value is measured
  or derived, and the vocabulary it uses, so that a consumer is never left inferring
  semantics from a column name.
\end{enumerate}

The same four steps admit shade measurements, periodontal charting, or a time series from
an intraoral sensor. This describes an extension route, not an implemented interoperable schema for each modality.
Shared periodontal and odontogram profiles, writers and independent readers remain to be
validated; inventing a different extension at each site would recreate fragmentation.

Declaring a modality is not validating it: UOS makes such a measurement carryable,
addressable by anatomy and traceable to whoever produced it, and says nothing about its
clinical meaning.

\section{A case, end to end}\label{sec:endtoend}

The argument so far is structural. This section walks one real case through the whole path,
because the value of a composition format is not visible in any single stage of it --- it
is visible in what the sixth reader can do that the first could not.

\paragraph{Source audit, 7 October 2026.} The walkthrough below records the August 2026
execution on case~A. An audit of the downloaded ZIP on 7 October 2026 found 401 instances in
two series and two reference frames. Four filenames occur in both series with different
contents; flattening the export into one directory overwrote four instances. The original
archive is preserved. Current preparation retains both series separately, and ingestion
rejects a mixed directory. Both series have nonuniform slice positions. Historical
registration and volume results from the mixed extraction cannot establish a coherent
single-series reconstruction or clinical accuracy.

\paragraph{1. Acquisition, unchanged.} The practice scans, takes the CBCT, shoots the
photographs and writes the report exactly as it does today. Nothing about UOS asks a
clinician to change how they work, and a format that did would not be adopted. The four
artefacts land as they always have: an STL, a DICOM export, nine image files and three
PDFs.

\paragraph{2. The container is written.} The pipeline ingests the four modalities, aligns
the CBCT to the scan, labels the teeth, composes the scene and writes the \code{.uos}.
The pipeline is invoked with one command; its ingestion stage, without fitting or export,
took at most 12.7\,s in August 2026 against a 60\,s budget. The acquired originals stay where
the clinic keeps them and are named inside the container by their content hash, the CBCT
export with a hash per file (397 files in the mixed extraction).

What the container added was the relations between the inputs: the CBCT-to-scanner transform
with its 0.666\,mm residual and the statement that nobody has verified it, the visit each
asset belongs to, per-vertex tooth codes in the removable inference plane, and the report's
findings anchored to the teeth they name. In that container per-Gaussian tooth codes were
also embedded in two PLY payloads declared processing layer~1; writer 0.6.0 moved them to
\code{derived/}, and that container was not re-exported.

\paragraph{3. It is validated without trusting the writer.} A reader runs the published
validator over the file and gets a report with check-derived codes. It covers whether the
manifest parses against the contract (schema conformance is reported as a warning), whether
carried assets are present and hash as declared, whether the frame graph is connected,
whether declared layer-3 assets and checked label payloads lie outside \code{derived/}, and
whether the provenance chain matches the manifests. The August container gave 0 errors,
conformance UOS-Core + UOS-Vol, and two warnings: one counting the 13 referenced originals
and one flagging the automatic, unverified CBCT-to-scanner registration. That validation
predates the check for tooth labels in PLY payloads, which the container would now fail.


\paragraph{4. A second reader inspects it.} The project's August 2026 demonstration reports a
browser viewer with layer controls, stored views and tooth cards. This repository exports
scene data, views and viewer-oriented PLY/JSON; it does not establish an independently
validated browser reader for arbitrary UOS files. A recipient needs a compatible reader
and separate access to any originals required for their task. Tooth highlighting depends
on experimental segmentation and does not validate anatomical boundaries.

\paragraph{5. Experimental laboratory exports.} Case~A's scan is a binary STL, which stores
\code{float32} coordinates, so regenerating it through the pipeline is exact by construction;
the $4.59\times10^{-6}$\,mm figure of \S\ref{sec:evidence} comes from decimal OBJ meshes of
a public corpus. Regional colour estimates and model-generated labels can be assigned to
that geometry, with the limitations in \S\ref{sec:negative}. A closed arch was produced
with 12{,}025 added faces in 3.2\,s in a single August 2026 run; those faces are synthetic.
Per-tooth exports combine a scanner-derived crown with a reconstructed root, and over-long
roots prevent laboratory use. Each composite STL has a \code{.provenance.json} companion
bound to its SHA-256, mapping triangle ranges to scanner geometry, synthetic closure or
reconstructed root. The map requires the unmodified STL and its companion; it does not
accompany a physical print or validate the inferred tooth assignments.

\paragraph{6. A later visit.} The format can record another visit and its registrations.
Actual change measurement still requires a stable reference, suitable landmarks and an
independent assessment of error. The provenance chain links versions and permits consistency
checks, but currently has no verified signatures to authenticate their authorship.

\begin{finding}{Longitudinal structure and longitudinal evidence}
Versioning and registration metadata are implemented. Reliable longitudinal clinical
measurement has not been established by this case. \S\ref{sec:roadmap} describes failures
in cross-modal and per-tooth measurements, including sensitivity to registration parameters.
\end{finding}

\section{What the recipient gets today}\label{sec:today}


\subsection{Geometry with regional appearance and explicit limitations}

Case~A's scan is an STL without colour, which is a property of that export rather than of
intraoral scanners in general. The prototype estimates CIELAB medians per crown third from
the clinical photographs and assigns those regional estimates to mesh vertices
(\S\ref{sec:evidence}). Vertices can instead carry a projected pixel, an interpolated value
or a fallback gradient, and the origin of each is recorded.

\begin{finding}{Photograph-derived estimates, not calibrated shade measurements}
The pipeline discounts flash falloff with a per-channel (linear RGB) Theil--Sen slope of log
tooth colour against log adjacent-gingiva colour, fitted across the photographs of a case,
clipped to $[0,1]$ and skipped when fewer than four crowns have a valid gingival probe. It
assumes that adjacent gingiva shares the tooth's illumination, it also removes part of any
genuine positional chroma gradient, and it leaves the absolute level uncalibrated. It
supports comparison between crowns of one case, not colourimetric accuracy or a shade match.
Camera processing, JPEG encoding and illumination affect the source, and colour measurement
is outside the intended use (\S\ref{sec:intended-use}).
\end{finding}

Tooth labels and closed-surface exports are available experimentally. Their limitations are
reported in \S\ref{sec:negative}, and surface closure inserts unmeasured geometry.

\subsection{Registration metadata for cross-modal measurements}

A distance between structures observed in different modalities depends on registration,
landmark extraction and acquisition errors. UOS records the fitted transform and its
residual, and provides optional fields for independently assessed TRE. It does not yet
compute a complete uncertainty budget or a validated cross-modal measurement.
The gingival-margin-to-alveolar-crest experiment in \S\ref{sec:rmeasure} remains
unsuccessful, and a fit residual such as case~A's 0.666\,mm must not be displayed as the
error bar of a cross-modal distance.

\subsection{A case that can be verified instead of trusted}


Carried assets have hashes checked against their bytes. A directory asset can declare a
per-file inventory; the validator tests embedded synthetic series for missing, altered and
extra files. Case~A's 397-file inventory was checked in August 2026, but the reference
writer leaves its original CBCT outside the ZIP. Validating that ZIP does not verify the
external series: the report explicitly counts external assets and warns that their contents
were not checked.

\subsection{Preservation with an external dependency}

JSON, public schemas and documented payload formats reduce dependence on one application.
Long-term recovery nevertheless requires retaining the referenced originals and the software
or specifications needed to interpret all payloads. UOS v0.3 does not implement an archival
profile that guarantees this. Optional locators do not guarantee continued access.

\subsection{An auditable research corpus}

Declared provenance can help researchers distinguish acquired and inferred inputs and track
model versions. Its usefulness depends on complete, correct declarations and validation of
the actual export path, including the clinical-layer limitation in \S\ref{sec:planes}.
This work does not establish that equivalent distinctions are absent from every other
public dental dataset, or that provenance alone prevents evaluation leakage.

\section{The representation choice: what Gaussian splatting adds, and what it does not}
\label{sec:gs}

UOS-Core can carry a mesh scene without Gaussian layers. The experimental pipeline also uses
3D Gaussian splatting \cite{kerbl2023}, with an attenuation variant for CBCT
\cite{xgaussian,r2gaussian}, as a common substrate for surface and volume data. This
section reports what that choice was measured to add. The volumetric figures come from three
CBCT volumes of a licensed research cohort, labelled V1--V3 to keep them apart from the
clinical cases; the seeding sweep and the regional control use one volume each. The fitting
code for these experiments is not versioned in the repository because of the cohort's
licence, so the figures are taken from the project's research record. They concern
representation and do not bear on diagnostic performance. The algebra is in
Appendix~\ref{app:math}.

\subsection{Re-fitting one acquisition does not create resolution}

We simulated a two-dimensional $9\times9$\,mm patch of occlusal surface with three findings,
each mainly on one support: an occlusal fissure of 0.15\,mm (geometry, encoded as a narrow
low-density, dark band), a white spot lesion of 0.60\,mm (surface colour, with a small
density reduction) and a dentinal lesion of 1.50\,mm under intact enamel (density only). The
CBCT chain applies the Gaussian point-spread function measured on case~B (FWHM 0.425\,mm),
sampling at 0.30\,mm and measured noise; the intraoral chains sample surface colour at their
pitch without a point-spread or noise model. Retained contrast is computed in closed form. At 0.15\,mm the
CBCT keeps about 2\,\% of the contrast (contrast-to-noise ratio about 0.1). A continuous
re-representation of the CBCT was emulated by interpolating its samples, not by fitting a
Gaussian field: it yields a smoother image carrying the same information.

\begin{finding}{Scope of this result}
A finding that exists only as density is invisible to a surface scanner, and a fissure
below the CBCT's resolution is not recovered by re-representing the same CBCT samples. Each
modality is simulated as one acquisition at one pitch, so the result does not cover
multi-view reconstruction, which can exceed the resolution of a single view when views
overlap with sub-pixel shifts.

The resolution gap between modalities is smaller than often assumed. On case~B, the
intraoral scan samples the arch surface at about 0.21\,mm, while the CBCT (0.30\,mm voxels)
has a measured edge-spread width of 0.425\,mm: roughly a factor of two, and the two numbers
measure different quantities. A representation-cost estimate gives an adaptive field a
$2.7\times$ advantage over a sparse grid at that scanner's resolution and none at CBCT
resolution. Gaussian layers are therefore a design choice for holding density, surface
colour and labels on one primitive, not a demonstrated efficiency gain.
\end{finding}

\subsection{Capacity observed with the chosen renderer}

Fitting a single field to one of the volumes and varying the seeding budget from 20{,}000 to 418{,}727
primitives, held-out projection PSNR stays between 40.2 and 43.2\,dB and does not improve
with the budget (Appendix~\ref{app:sweep}). The live set settles at about 8{,}000--11{,}000
primitives: after global calibration of the density scale, the same total attenuation is
spread over more primitives and a growing fraction falls below the renderer's opacity
threshold. The plateau depends on the dynamic range of the target image; V2 keeps 22{,}887
live primitives in a single field.

The experiments use the \code{gsplat} rasteriser. It skips a primitive at a pixel when its
alpha there is below $1/255$, clamps each primitive's alpha at 0.999 and stops compositing a
pixel before its transmittance falls to $10^{-4}$, which bounds the expressible line
integral (Appendix~\ref{app:ceiling}). These are implementation thresholds, not properties
of Gaussian representations. Splitting each volume into five layers, each with its own
density calibration, raised held-out PSNR on all three volumes
(Table~\ref{tab:layers}).

\begin{finding}{The layer gain did not transfer to case~A}
The comparison was repeated on case~A's CBCT with the same protocol (0.30\,mm resampling,
252 views, one in eight held out), before the source audit of \S\ref{sec:endtoend}, and
therefore on the extraction that mixed two series. Four layers defined by density range
alone scored 39.83\,dB against 37.35\,dB for one field ($+2.48$\,dB). Four layers that
crossed the project's binary tooth segmentation with density ranges scored 36.07\,dB against
37.32\,dB ($-1.25$\,dB): tissue the segmenter misses falls into a layer with the wrong
density scale. A density-only partition was then added to the export pipeline and
withdrawn the same day, because its layers grouped enamel with metal and soft tissue with
trabecular bone and so carried no anatomical meaning. The reference writer emits one
CBCT-derived density field, a CBCT--scanner composite and a scanner appearance layer; it
emits no tissue layers.
\end{finding}

\subsection{What this section commits the format to}

Two design decisions in \cite{uosspec} follow. A Gaussian layer in a container declares
whether it is \textbf{measured} or \textbf{reconstructed}, and the appearance layer is
declared reconstructed (\S\ref{sec:negative}). A layer declared measured must state the
occupancy threshold that decided which tissue appears in it. The density field in case~A's
container is the unfitted seed produced at ingestion; the projection scores in this section
come from separate fitting experiments and do not describe that field.

A fitted field produces visually smoother output than the voxels it was fitted to. That
smoothness comes from the representation's prior, not from recovered signal, and any
denoising claim would need a task-level evaluation against ground truth, which this work
does not include.

\subsection{Source-separated repeatability experiment, October 2026}

The corrected runner was executed three times independently for each of the two original
series: six runs in total. Both series were kept separate and referenced by per-instance
hashes. The protocol included ingestion, automatic registration, Gaussian density fitting,
export and mesh regeneration, with seed 0 and a fixed local software environment.
Existing IOS labels and the archived appearance were reused. The CBCT segmentation model
was excluded because these inputs are not regular volumetric grids; no missing slices were
interpolated. This is a repeatability experiment for that protocol, not for new appearance
training or clinical segmentation.

All source audits passed. Within each series, all eight binary payloads, processing records
and registration matrices were identical across the three runs. Container bytes differed;
JSON payload bytes were inventoried separately, without claiming semantic equivalence.
The regenerated STL and PLY outputs were identical across all six runs. These observations
are limited to the tested input, configuration and environment; they are not a guarantee
for other hardware or software versions.

The 100-instance series gave a 16.830\,mm registration residual with no accepted overlap.
The 301-instance series gave 0.680\,mm on only 3.6\,\% overlap and 8.341\,mm across the
whole cloud. Neither result establishes clinically correct alignment. A numerical check
against the source headers found maximum coordinate discrepancies below
$2\times10^{-6}$\,mm for the transported seed positions. This verifies coordinate
conservation, not acquisition accuracy or physical calibration. The original DICOM frame
and the centred field are separate frames, with their relationship explicitly composed
from the recorded origin and ICP transform.

The runs used Python 3.13.9, NumPy 2.5.1, SciPy 1.18.0, PyTorch 2.11.0 with CUDA 12.8 and
an NVIDIA RTX 5070. At that verification the implementation suite passed 1{,}175 tests, with
two skipped and two pre-existing numerical warnings. The writer version was 0.18.0 and the
container format UOS 0.3. These six runs predate the per-run record described at the start
of this section.

A registration restricted to slices for sparse series was added on 9 November 2026. It
starts from anatomical axes (FDI labels on the scan, patient axes in the DICOM header) and
fits CBCT tooth contours to the scan within a band 1.5--6\,mm above the occlusal plane. On
the 301-instance series of case~A, the median distance from scan crown vertices to CBCT
enamel fell from 4.92 to 1.59\,mm, and the pose moved by about 2\,mm (median) across three
contour thresholds. That check reuses the same DICOM and is one-sided: in case~B a check of
the same kind scored two poses 2\,mm apart equally. It is not a TRE. \todo{Registration accuracy for cases A and
B: landmark TRE pending specialist annotations; update after the robust-registration
protocol is frozen and run.}

\subsection{Appearance estimated from clinical photographs}

In the archived appearance run of case~A (August 2026), 108{,}922 of 112{,}067 vertices
(97.2\,\%) take a regional colour: the median of their crown third in the photograph that
sees that crown best, for 13 of the 14 crowns. These are regional summaries, not
independent per-vertex samples. The remaining 3{,}145 vertices, according to the output
header recorded in the test suite, comprise 7 with a projected pixel, 97 interpolated from
the nearest measured vertex and 3{,}041 painted with a fallback gradient that is not patient
colour; the run log itself was not archived. A later run without per-tooth shade extraction
projected colour onto 86{,}142 vertices and interpolated 14{,}004, so the coverage figure
belongs to that archived run.

The flash-correction slopes recorded for case~A are 0.649 / 0.577 / 0.677. They come from the
specification's example record; the producing run log was not archived, and the code
documentation gives 0.63 / 0.59 / 0.69 for an earlier iteration on the same case. They
describe a processing step, not colourimetric accuracy. The optional fidelity extension
identifies JPEG encoding from the file bytes and records it as lossy, together with per-asset
lineage and inherited losses, and keeps prior history unknown unless evidence is supplied.

\subsection{The gingival boundary in the photographs}

An Otsu threshold on the CIELAB $a^\star$ channel of four full-arch photographs of case~A
gives a two-class partition in each view, with a standardised distance of 3.4--4.3 between
the two classes (Appendix~\ref{app:otsu}). Because Otsu chooses the threshold that maximises
that separation, the figure shows that the $a^\star$ histogram is bimodal; it does not show
that the two modes are tooth and gingiva. On visual inspection the mask edge follows the
gingival scallop, with false positives on a plastic retractor, specular highlights and the
tongue. After projecting colour onto scan vertices with estimated camera poses, $a^\star$
differed between the pipeline's tooth and gingiva labels by 0.85 pooled standard deviations
overall and 0.61 per tooth. Relocating the crown boundary with the colour mask reduced the
mean excess crown width from 2.36 to 1.94\,mm, but 9 of 14 crowns remained too wide. Both
comparisons use the pipeline's own labels, not an anatomical reference: colour carries a
weak boundary signal in the expected direction, and its localisation accuracy is unknown.

\section{What did not work, and what it cost the format}\label{sec:negative}

Three lines of work did not reach the outcome they were set up for. Each is reported with
the criterion it was judged against.

\subsection{Per-tooth segmentation on the intraoral scan}

\paragraph{Identity.} In August 2026 the pipeline assigned 14 FDI codes to case~A's
14-tooth maxillary arch in monotone arch order, but order is not identity: against
clinician-reviewed tooth curves, 7 were correct and 4 were shifted by one position. Voting
among the three most confident of 18 canonicalisation references later numbered 12 of 14 as
the reviewed curves, with none shifted. On 80 held-out Teeth3DS+ arches \cite{teeth3ds} the
same voting numbered 979 of 1{,}065 teeth correctly, against 1{,}017 in the dataset's native
pose, and failed three of six criteria fixed before the run.

\paragraph{Boundaries.} Without ground truth, 11 of case~A's 14 crowns failed an anatomical
plausibility test in August 2026: bounding-box size against tabulated crown dimensions
(threshold 1.30, not calibrated) or contralateral asymmetry. That bounds correctly delimited
crowns at 21\,\%. Such a test can reject a crown but not confirm one; with a width tolerance
calibrated on expert-labelled crowns ($\pm1.9$\,mm, their 95th percentile), 4 of 14 fall
within range. The learned segmenter placed the crown--gingiva margin where the surface shows
no crease. A geometric method that moves the margin to the cervical crease
(\code{ancla\_al\_pliegue}) was then evaluated once on 80 held-out Teeth3DS+ arches: the
median margin distance to the expert fell from 2.42 to 0.17\,mm, but crown labelled as
gingiva rose by 1.51 points against a pre-registered limit of 1.0, so the method is disabled
by default. On case~A, against a margin line traced blind by one periodontist, the default
pipeline's margin lay a median 3.53\,mm away and the method's 0.19\,mm. The blind line and a
second annotation (proposed by software and reviewed by another dentist) lie 0.15\,mm apart,
which is the floor of this measurement. This is one case and one blind annotator. A later variant
for teeth the method declines was chosen using these same two annotations, which therefore no
longer measure it; confirming it needs a new blind annotation.

\paragraph{Consequence for the format.} Tooth labels come from a model, so they belong to
the inference plane, while picking in a generic glTF viewer is defined over per-primitive
metadata in the scene. Writer version 0.4.0 split the mesh into one primitive per tooth so
that any glTF viewer could select one; that placed model output inside an asset declared
processing layer~1, and deleting \code{derived/} no longer removed the inference. The current
writer keeps the mesh unpartitioned, the labels live only in \code{derived/}, and a UOS-aware
reader rebuilds picking from the vertex index \cite{uosspec}. A generic glTF viewer can open
the scene but cannot select a tooth.

\subsection{A more precise classifier did not shorten the roots}

\paragraph{What we measure.} On case~B (both arches), the composite CBCT$+$scan export
delivers each tooth with a reconstructed root, and about half the pieces come out too long,
up to 40.5\,mm where anatomical tables give 20--25\,mm for a whole tooth; the excess is
alveolar bone below the apex. The first CBCT tooth segmenter had recall 0.948 and precision
0.588 on whole ToothFairy2 volumes \cite{toothfairy2}, so a more precise model was expected to
trim the bone. A second model raised volume-level precision to 0.724 (F1 from 0.726 to 0.822)
but left the longest piece at 40.2\,mm, at every decision threshold from 0.5 to 0.9. It also named fewer teeth (20
against 27), because the registration target was then derived from the segmentation mask;
once that coupling was removed both models named 27 teeth, and the over-long roots remained.

\paragraph{What follows.} Voxel-level precision and apex localisation are different
objectives, and the benchmark rewarded the first. A table-length truncation was tried: along
one arch axis it barely trimmed inclined molars, and the per-tooth axis estimated from the CBCT
deviated by about $60^\circ$, so it was withdrawn and the exporter declares over-long roots
instead of trimming them. At case~B's 0.30\,mm voxels the periodontal ligament that separates root from bone is not
sampled (\S\ref{sec:rmeasure}). A truncation criterion
evaluated on tooth length rather than voxel overlap remains open.

\subsection{Sharpness of the appearance field}

\paragraph{What we measure.} The appearance field reproduces its training views at
34--36\,dB PSNR and still looks soft. The project's sharpness measure is the
\emph{footprint}, the major axis of the median Gaussian in $\mu$m, with a criterion
of $<563\,\mu$m fixed \emph{before} running. Across seedings from 113\,000 to 1\,090\,000
primitives the criterion is not met, and more primitives do not move it: the median
footprint measures 845.9\,$\mu$m, 46.5\,\% of primitives sit more than 1\,mm from the
surface, and the field is anisotropic by $3.0\times$.

\paragraph{What was tried.} The first runs used flat-albedo supervision: training views
were renders of the mesh painted with the regional colour summaries of \S\ref{sec:evidence}.
Against such a target the image loss cannot distinguish a large diffuse primitive from a
small sharp one. Three training variants failed the criterion: depth supervision with axis
ratio flattening (1{,}344\,$\mu$m), MCMC densification with antialiasing (827\,$\mu$m, with
an unstable spatial extent) and 2048\,px renders with 1.09\,M primitives (34.15\,dB,
906\,$\mu$m). Supervision with the photographs' own pixels was then tested. Camera poses
within the 0.9\,mm gate were obtained for two of the nine images, giving projected colour on
76.2\,\% of vertices. Retraining reached 35.97\,dB with a median footprint of
842.4\,$\mu$m; in the occlusal region, where the new texture landed, the footprint changed
from 870.6 to 866.9\,$\mu$m. The criterion was not met. The diffuse result is therefore not
explained by the flat target alone in this experiment, and it does not establish a general
limit of Gaussian splatting. Supervision with validated poses for most of the arch, and a
direct penalty on the median major axis, remain untested.

These results determine one declaration in the format: appearance layers are declared
\emph{reconstructed}, not \emph{measured}.

\section{Intended use and regulatory posture}\label{sec:regulatory}

\paragraph{Intended use.}\label{sec:intended-use} A draft intended-use statement (version
0.1, 10 October 2026, to be ratified with a specialist before any evaluation) fixes the task
before it is evaluated. A dentist or specialist reviewing a case already diagnosed by the
usual means opens the CBCT, the intraoral scan and the report in one scene, selects a tooth
and sees what each source says about it, with the provenance of each value and inferred
values marked as such. The context is investigational; the user keeps their usual method and
decides with it. Excluded are diagnosis, treatment planning or any clinical decision taken
with UOS; measurement of roots, bone or anatomical distances on the combined scene; planning
of implants, surgical guides or fabrication; reading grey values as calibrated Hounsfield
units; and colour measurement. The statement sets provisional acceptance criteria, among
them a maximum landmark TRE of 1.5\,mm and an overlap fraction of at least 0.20 for the
registration. Neither is met in case~A: TRE has not been measured and the overlap
is 0--3.6\,\%. The statement does not determine the system's regulatory classification.


The layer mechanism describes provenance and supports separating model outputs from other
assets. It does not determine whether software is a medical device. Under the European
software guidance, qualification depends on intended purpose; deterministic processing may
also qualify when it serves a medical purpose~\cite{mdcg}. Removing \code{derived/} therefore
does not by itself establish that the remaining software or case may be distributed or used
for diagnosis. No clinical clearance is claimed here.

The implementation computes a \code{UOS-Distributable} metadata profile from declarations
about de-identification, consent, purpose and processing layers. This is a technical check
of stated conditions, not a legal authorisation, an independent de-identification audit,
or a guarantee that referenced originals are accessible. The issuer remains responsible
for the actual data, access arrangements and applicable use restrictions.

\begin{finding}{Recorded fidelity evidence does not establish clinical suitability}
The reference implementation supplies the optional \code{uos\_fidelity\_provenance}
extension, version 1.0, without changing the UOS 0.3 format version. It records source
lineage, known losses, inherited unknown history, processing and scoped claims. Absence of
evidence remains explicit; no diagnostic eligibility is inferred from format validation.
Volume metadata includes spacing, orientation, encoding and actual slice positions. The
writer emits \code{calibrated\_hu: false} together with
\code{calibration\_status: unknown}: calibration has not been established by the writer.
Modality and rescale do not constitute evidence of calibrated Hounsfield units.

The downloaded CBCT declares \code{RescaleType=HU}, slope 1 and intercept $-1000$;
these are provider declarations, not an independent calibration experiment. Nonuniform
slice sampling is preserved and warned, without filling unobserved intervals. The Gaussian
results in this paper are representation experiments; no diagnostic task has been validated
on them. Geometry and colour calibration also remain unestablished.
\end{finding}

Signatures, encryption profiles, authenticated clinical review and signed redaction are not
implemented as a complete lifecycle. Existing declarations and hash checks do not provide
those guarantees.

The provenance chain & FHIR \code{Provenance} is an intended mapping target: which entity, generated by
which agent, when, superseding which.\\
Image selections & FHIR R5's \code{ImagingSelection} is a candidate for selections in
imaging data. An actual connector must verify source identifiers, supported references
and anatomical semantics; it is not an implemented mapping here.\\
The scene & Binary \textbf{glTF} with optional Gaussian layers. The upstream
\code{KHR\_gaussian\_splatting} specification was listed as ratified when consulted on
20 November 2026~\cite{khrgs}; the reference implementation was written against the release
candidate. Compatibility with the ratified text and retirement of fallback payloads require
explicit testing.\\
The clinical findings & A coding structure \code{\{system, code, display\}} with unassigned SNOMED CT codes and
tooth numbering to ISO 3950 \cite{iso3950}.\\
\bottomrule
\end{longtable}

\begin{finding}{Where a code is deliberately left empty}
Findings carry a coding structure, but the SNOMED \code{code} is \code{null}; they are not
yet terminology-coded findings. Assigning one is
clinical terminology work, and a guessed code is indistinguishable from a correct one to
the connector that resolves it. The FHIR mapping follows the same rule: UOS declares the
resource \emph{type} (\code{Media} for the photographs, \code{ImagingStudy} for the volume,
\code{DocumentReference} for the mesh, the reports and the case, \code{Observation} for the
clinical and inferred layers) and leaves the reference empty, because the case has not been
through any practice-management system and there is no identifier to cite.
\end{finding}


The current photo resource hint is \code{Media}, consistent with FHIR R4. A future R5
connector would use \code{DocumentReference} for this media role; volume references use
\code{ImagingStudy}, and mesh/case attachments can use \code{DocumentReference} in either
version~\cite{fhir}. No bidirectional, version-specific connector is demonstrated here.

DICOM already represents acquisitions and several relations UOS needs. The contribution UOS
proposes is its packaging, scene and provenance conventions; DICOM round-trip mappings have
not been tested.

\section{Status, and what openness means here}\label{sec:status}

A format is a claim about the future, so it is worth being exact about what exists today and
what is a statement of intent.

\begin{longtable}{L{4.6cm} L{10.8cm}}
\toprule
\textbf{Artefact} & \textbf{Status}\\
\midrule
\endhead
\textbf{The specification} & \emph{UOS Format Specification v0.3} \cite{uosspec}: a draft,
with the container, the data model, the manifest field by field, the conformance levels, the
validation algorithm and a cookbook of implementation recipes.\\
\textbf{The JSON Schemas} & A manifest schema and a validation-report schema per format
version, and one for the optional fidelity-provenance extension 1.0. The manifest and
extension schemas are generated from the implementation's models; the report schema is
written by hand and checked against the implementation in CI. A schema alone does not
validate the writer or the payload semantics.\\
\textbf{The validator} & Open source, with numbered checks whose findings carry a
check-derived code (\code{UOS-E-005}). The codes have not yet been reconciled with the
specification's check table for every check, and some codes cover more than one rule.\\
\textbf{The conformance fixture} & Thirteen containers: two expected valid, nine expected errors, one expected warning,
and one exercising forward compatibility --- with the expected verdict and the
reason stated beside each. Built by running the real writer over synthetic data, and
re-verified by the test suite on every run.\\
\textbf{The reference implementation} & A Python package that writes, reads and validates,
and the experimental pipeline. Figures obtained before 10 October 2026 have no
machine-written run record, and not every measurement has been reproduced.\\
\textbf{The licence} & Apache~2.0, covering the document and the reference implementation
alike.\\
\textbf{The media type} & \code{application/vnd.histora.uos}, \textbf{proposed and not
registered}.\\
\bottomrule
\end{longtable}

\paragraph{How the document changes, and who decides.} Changes are opened as issues and pull
requests against the public repository that hosts both this source and the implementation. A
change to the format and the change to the implementation that realises it belong in the same
request --- a proposal that does not say how it would be validated is not yet a proposal ---
and each must state the rule, where a validator would check it and at what severity, and
what it does to a container written under the previous version. The specification's editor,
currently the author, decides in the open, on the record of the request.

\paragraph{How a version is published.} The manifest and validation-report schema
identifiers are pinned to a git tag rather than to a branch, so that an identifier always
returns the same content. A version under development needs an identifier that resolves
while its content changes, and a published version one that never changes, so each version
has two tags. The pre-release pointer \code{uos-spec-v0.3-draft} is named by the identifiers
while the specification is a draft and may move. The release tag \code{uos-spec-v0.3} is
created once at publication and never moved; it does not exist yet. The fidelity-extension
schema has the non-resolvable identifier \code{uos-fidelity-provenance/1.0} and is published
alongside the others at the pre-release tag.

\begin{finding}{What ``open'' does and does not provide}
UOS is a proposal with one implementation and no governing body, not a standard. Two steps
would change that, in order: a \textbf{second, independent implementation} that opens
containers written by the first, without which interoperability has not been demonstrated;
and a \textbf{registered media type} with the schemas hosted at a domain the project
controls. Openness already allows a recipient to validate a container without trusting its
writer and an integrator to implement a reader from the specification, provided the payloads
and external originals remain available.
\end{finding}

\section{Limitations}\label{sec:limitations}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Two clinical cases, no ground truth.} The clinical figures come from two
  patients; the Gaussian-field results come from three volumes of a licensed cohort that
  cannot be redistributed. None of this is a clinical study. Neither case has landmark-based
  ground truth for registration, and the only clinical annotations are margin lines on
  case~A, two of which have already been used to choose a method. Fit residuals and
  reconstruction scores do not bound clinical error.
  \item \textbf{No distributable fixture of a real case.} The conformance fixture bank is
  synthetic. The measured container holds a real patient's case and is not published, so an
  implementer can check a reader against the fixtures but not against a clinical container.
  \item \textbf{Signals are unimplemented.} The asset class exists; no writer emits one, so
  the time-series path of \S\ref{sec:newmodality} is designed but untested.
  \item \textbf{Signatures are unspecified.} What is missing is not code but the decision
  of which key signs and where it lives; signing with an invented key would produce a
  container that appears signed.
  \item \textbf{The clinical act is not recorded.} The container says which software and
  which model produced a datum, and whether a human verified an alignment. It does not say
  who performed the study, under what protocol. That is a different axis from software
  provenance, and \S\ref{sec:roadmap} explains why it is a design decision rather than a
  field.
  \item \textbf{One implementation, unregistered media type.} Interoperability across
  independent implementations has not been tested (\S\ref{sec:status}).
\end{itemize}

\section{Roadmap and outlook}\label{sec:roadmap}

This section lists measurements requested by the collaborating clinicians and what the
results above imply about reaching them.

\subsection{The measurement clinicians actually want}\label{sec:rmeasure}

Unless stated otherwise, the measurements in this section come from case~B, whose CBCT is
dense, and are single-patient results.

Validating whether a vertical gingiva-to-enamel dimension of the kind shown in commercial
2D demos is reproducible on a composed case turned out to reveal that it is not one measurement but
\textbf{three}, and that they differ in what they need and in what they are worth.

\begin{longtable}{C{0.6cm} L{5.4cm} L{3.2cm} L{5.2cm}}
\toprule
& \textbf{Measurement} & \textbf{Needs} & \textbf{Status}\\
\midrule
\endhead
1 & Gingival margin $\to$ incisal edge & Scanner only & \textbf{Available.} Everything
happens inside a single mesh within the scan, with no cross-modal registration; landmark and acquisition error remain
to be assessed. This is the well-conditioned rung and it is implemented.\\
2 & Cemento-enamel junction $\to$ alveolar crest & CBCT only & Blocked on locating the CEJ
(\S\ref{sec:cej}).\\
3 & Gingival margin $\to$ alveolar crest & Scanner \emph{and} CBCT & The cross-modal
measurement. Blocked on identifying the crest at the available CBCT resolution, and
dependent on a registration error that has not been measured.\\
\bottomrule
\end{longtable}

Rung 3 is where the argument of this document meets clinical practice. It cannot be taken
from a radiograph, because the gingival margin is soft tissue and does not appear on one; and
it cannot be taken from a scan, because the alveolar crest is under the gingiva. The experiment combines the two sources; it has not established superiority over other
clinical measurement workflows.

\begin{finding}{Rung 3 does not yet produce a measurement}
On case~B's mandibular arch (whole-arch registration RMS about 0.49\,mm), rays were cast
apically from 21 detected gingival-margin sites. On the buccal side 1 of 21 rays reached
mineralised tissue. On the lingual side 6 of 21 did, but the detected crest moved by
3.4--8.7\,mm when the ray offset changed by 1.5\,mm, which fails a stability criterion fixed
before the run. The nearest hard tissue lay 0.7--1.05\,mm coronal to each margin and is
attributed to the tooth rather than to bone. Separating root from crest requires the
periodontal ligament (0.15--0.38\,mm wide), which 0.30\,mm voxels do not sample.

The documented limit is therefore crest identification at this resolution; tighter
registration alone would not remove it. A better-resolved acquisition and anatomical
criteria for the crest would be needed. Separately, a whole-arch scanner-to-CBCT
registration of case~B's maxilla gives an RMS of 0.452\,mm over the 27\,\% of scanner points
within 1\,mm of CBCT enamel (4.98\,mm over the whole cloud). Its positive control, which
should return zero, returns 0.486\,mm, so the procedure has no clean floor.
\end{finding}

\subsection{What is measurably out of reach, and what replaces it}\label{sec:cej}

Absolute gingival recession is defined against the cemento-enamel junction (CEJ). In
exploratory analyses on case~B we could not locate the CEJ reliably: in the CBCT, restorative
metal dominates its neighbourhood and cervical enamel is thinner than the voxel, and a
shape-based estimate depended strongly on the chosen threshold; on the scan, the cervical step
was below the triangulation noise. Those analyses are not reproducible from the repository,
so no figures are given. The gingival margin itself is detectable on the scan, which makes a
change of margin position between two registered visits a candidate quantity. Its detection
threshold would come from same-session null controls such as the one in
\S\ref{sec:pertooth}; no clinical threshold has been established.

\subsection{Per-tooth motion, and a reference that does not move}\label{sec:pertooth}

Orthodontic follow-up asks a narrower question: did \emph{this} tooth move, and by how much.
On two intraoral scans of case~B's mandibular arch (scanner to scanner, no CBCT), per-tooth
rigid registration lowered the held-out residual relative to the whole-arch registration in
13 of 13 teeth. The displacement it yields depends on the reference frame. Measured against
the whole-arch registration, the median displacement moved from \textbf{0.171 to
0.738\,mm} when only the ICP trimming parameter changed: every tooth appeared to move
because the frame moved. Re-estimating the frame from all teeth except the target
(leave-one-out) reduced this to 0.158--0.182\,mm, and the largest per-tooth change to
0.107\,mm. Two independent scans from the same session, between which no tooth moved, gave
displacements of up to 0.39--0.54\,mm, so displacements below about 0.4\,mm are not
distinguishable from zero with this procedure. The leave-one-out frame assumes that the other
teeth are stable.

Averaging is measurably worse than not correcting. Estimating the transform for three teeth
and applying their average to the others was worse than the whole-arch registration in
2{,}071 of 2{,}860 evaluations (all 286 triples of 13 teeth) when the rotation was averaged
about each tooth's centroid, and in 2{,}629 when the matrices were averaged directly.

\subsection{Modalities that are not geometry}

\paragraph{Per-site microbial measurements.} This is the instance the extension mechanism of
\S\ref{sec:newmodality} was written for. Three requirements follow, and only the first is
met today: the value must be addressable by anatomical site, which the container supports;
it must be located in time, which the visit mechanism supports but no writer exercises; and
it must be declared with its detection limit, because a reported zero and a concentration
below the assay's threshold are different facts.

\paragraph{Occlusal force.} Clinicians also asked for bite force distributed over the
occlusal surface rather than as one number per patient. Mechanically this is the same shape
of problem as per-tooth colour: a scalar sampled at points and interpolated onto a surface the
container already carries. Two properties make it more delicate. A false-colour overlay must
never be mistakable for measured appearance, so a force map has to declare itself as a mapped
quantity with its own units rather than as an appearance layer. And the colour map is
\emph{part of the datum}: a field rendered through an unstated palette is not reproducible,
because the same measurements under a different scale tell a different clinical story.

\subsection{Audit: who did this, and how}

The container answers which software produced a datum, with which model and weights, and
whether a human verified an alignment. It does not answer what a clinician asks when a case
arrives from elsewhere: who performed this study, under what protocol, and when.

\begin{finding}{Why this cannot simply be a name field}
The obvious implementation, a professional's name on the acquisition, collides with the
property the format works hardest to preserve. A clinician is a person, their identity is
personal data, and a container that pseudonymises the patient while naming the practitioner
in clear has moved the problem rather than solved it.

So the work is a design decision with three parts: an actor identifier that can be resolved
by an institution and not by a recipient; a protocol reference that names how an acquisition
was performed rather than who performed it, since that is what a second reader needs in order
to reproduce it; and an extension of the declared PHI state to say whether professional
identities are present, so that the declaration made for the patient covers every person in
the record. Signatures are the natural carrier for the first, and are themselves
unspecified for the same reason: the missing piece is a decision about keys and custody, not
code.
\end{finding}

\subsection{Treatment planning is outside the current scope}

Graft and implant planning would need bone volume available and required, separated from
soft tissue and from restorative metal, each with a stated error. This work does not provide
it: the writer emits no tissue layers, and the decompositions tried on case~A either lost
projection quality or carried no anatomical meaning (\S\ref{sec:gs}). Treatment planning and measurement of roots, bone or
anatomical distances on the combined scene are excluded by the intended use
(\S\ref{sec:intended-use}). Including them would need multi-class tissue segmentation, a new
intended-use statement with its own acceptance criteria, and comparison with an independent
clinical reference.

\subsection{Platform work}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{A rasteriser that integrates $\sigma$ directly}, without the opacity
  threshold and early termination of the photometric rasteriser used here. Its effect on
  quality and numerical stability needs a controlled comparison; it does not remove the need
  to characterise acquisition calibration.
  \item \textbf{A second implementation and a realistic distributable fixture.} A reader
  written independently of the reference writer, tested against a fixture of a realistic case
  built from an openly licensed dataset.
  \item \textbf{Root truncation scored on tooth length} rather than voxel overlap
  (\S\ref{sec:negative}).
  \item \textbf{Photographic colour as a second boundary signal.} A first mask-based
  relocation narrowed crowns by 0.42\,mm on average without bringing any over-wide crown into
  tolerance (\S\ref{sec:evidence}). A fused method needs camera poses, access to the
  referenced photographs and labelled boundaries for evaluation.
  \item \textbf{A controlled colour capture and calibration study}, including suitable references
  and evaluation of residual error before claiming calibrated shade measurements.
  \item \textbf{A non-geometric modality end to end}, microbial load or periodontal
  charting, to test \S\ref{sec:newmodality} against a real dataset rather than a design.
  \item \textbf{Ratified-extension compatibility.} Compare the implementation against the
  ratified \code{KHR\_gaussian\_splatting} specification before changing the external-layer
  fallback or claiming compatibility with the final extension.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}

UOS describes a multimodal dental case as one composition: acquisitions referenced by hash,
spatial relations with their residuals, per-asset processing provenance and presentation
state, with model output separable from the rest. A specification, schemas, a reference
implementation and synthetic conformance fixtures exist.

The measurements bound what can be claimed. Mesh regeneration preserves coordinates to
\code{float32} rounding. Registration residuals describe the fit, not target accuracy: in
case~A the historical residual came from mixed series, and in case~B the residual did not
detect a $180^\circ$ flip. Tooth labels, regional colour and reconstructed roots remain
experimental. The Gaussian experiments improved projection scores on a research cohort but
not on case~A with segmented classes. No landmark-based registration error exists yet for
either case, and independent calibration, independent readers and clinical evaluation remain
to be done.

\section{Mathematical formulation}\label{app:math}

This appendix describes the mathematical models used in the experiments. Idealised
identities are distinguished from renderer-specific thresholds and empirical results;
none is a clinical validation of the fitted representation.

\subsection{The case as a graph}\label{app:graph}

The formal object UOS stores is a labelled graph. Let $F$ be the set of coordinate frames
named in a case and let one distinguished frame $f_0 \in F$, conventionally the intraoral
scanner's, be canonical. Every asset $a$ declares exactly one frame $\phi(a) \in F$.
Every registration is an edge carrying a rigid transform

\[
  T_{ij} \;=\;
  \begin{pmatrix} R_{ij} & t_{ij} \\ \mathbf{0}^{\!\top} & 1 \end{pmatrix}
  \;\in\; SE(3), \qquad R_{ij} \in SO(3),\; t_{ij} \in \mathbb{R}^3 ,
\]

mapping homogeneous points from frame $i$ into frame $j$, in millimetres, right-handed.
Placing an asset in the canonical frame is composition along any path to $f_0$,

\[
  T_{i \to 0} \;=\; T_{i_k 0} \, T_{i_{k-1} i_k} \cdots T_{i i_1}.
\]

A path must exist for a placement to be defined. Connectivity alone does not make all
paths equivalent: the reference implementation chooses a shortest path, breaking ties by
registration identifiers, and warns about discrepancies between alternatives.

\subsection{Registration as an estimator with a reported error}\label{app:icp}

The CBCT-to-scanner edge is estimated by multiscale point-to-point ICP. Let
$\{p_i\}_{i=1}^{N}$ be the scanner vertices (source) and $Q$ the CBCT Gaussian centres
retained by an intensity threshold (target), in millimetres. In the production path the
source is a seeded random subsample of 4\,000 scanner vertices, and the threshold is chosen
among several candidates by registering against each and scoring the result against CBCT
enamel. At iteration $k$, with
estimate $T_k$, each source point is paired with its nearest target point,
\[
  c_i = \arg\min_{q\in Q}\lVert T_k p_i - q\rVert, \qquad d_i = \lVert T_k p_i - c_i\rVert,
\]
and a set $A_k$ keeps the $\lceil \rho N\rceil$ smallest $d_i$, with trimming fraction
$\rho\in(0,1]$ ($\rho=1$ is classical ICP). The update
\[
  (\Delta R,\Delta t) \;=\; \arg\min_{R\in SO(3),\,t\in\mathbb{R}^3}
  \sum_{i\in A_k}\big\lVert R\,T_k p_i + t - c_i\big\rVert^2
\]
has the closed-form Kabsch solution: with centroids $\bar x,\bar c$ of the paired points and
$H=\sum_{i\in A_k}(T_k p_i-\bar x)(c_i-\bar c)^{\!\top}=U S V^{\!\top}$,
$\Delta R = V\,\mathrm{diag}\big(1,1,\det(VU^{\!\top})\big)\,U^{\!\top}$ and
$\Delta t=\bar c-\Delta R\,\bar x$. Iterations run on random subsamples of 10, 35 and
100\,\% of the source and stop when the RMS over $A_k$ changes by less than
$10^{-6}$\,mm. At the converged pose $\hat T$, with distances $d_i$ over all source points,
the implementation reports the overlap set $O=\{i : d_i<1\,\mathrm{mm}\}$, the overlap
fraction $f=|O|/N$ and
\[
  \varepsilon_{\mathrm{RMS}} \;=\; \sqrt{\tfrac{1}{|O|}\textstyle\sum_{i\in O} d_i^{2}}
\]
(the full-cloud RMS when $O$ is empty). The review-gate confidence is $0$ if $f<0.2$ and
otherwise $\mathrm{clamp}(1-\varepsilon_{\mathrm{RMS}}/\varepsilon,0,1)$, with
$\varepsilon=1.5$\,mm for intraoral--CBCT pairs. $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{RMS}}$ is the
minimised point-to-point criterion restricted to the overlapping points; it is not a TRE.
In case~B, poses rotated by about $180^\circ$ reached the same $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{RMS}}$
as correct ones (\S\ref{sec:relations}), and only $f$ separated them.


\subsection{Identity of a directory asset}\label{app:hash}

Whole-file SHA-256 hashes identify exact bytes. For a directory, the current implementation
uses a conditional digest. If every part has a SOP Instance UID $u_k$ and a PixelData hash
$p_k$, it computes
\[
 H_{\mathrm{series}} = H\!\left(\big\Vert_{k\in\mathrm{sort}(u)}
 (u_k\Vert\texttt{NUL}\Vert p_k\Vert\texttt{LF})\right).
\]
Otherwise it uses sorted filenames and whole-file hashes with the same separators.
The strings are UTF-8 and the hashes are lowercase hexadecimal, as implemented by
\code{digesto\_de\_partes}. Renaming does not change the UID-based digest; it changes the
fallback digest. Filenames are locators, not anatomical slice-order evidence.

The PixelData hash covers the stored element value, not a canonical decoded image plus
geometry and scaling attributes. It is therefore not invariant to every lossless transfer
syntax change and does not by itself check geometric equivalence. UID changes during
specified de-identification workflows also need explicit handling. Per-frame multiframe
identity and a canonical semantic hash remain unimplemented. For external originals,
container validation reports that their content was not checked. For embedded series, a
whole-file hash or size mismatch fails validation even when UID and stored PixelData
match. Rewritten DICOM requires a regenerated manifest; semantic equivalence is not assumed.

\subsection{Notation}
The model borrows from two literatures whose conventions collide on
one letter, and we keep both rather than renaming, so that a reader arriving from either
finds the symbol where they expect it. Lowercase $\sigma_i$ is the \emph{density} of
primitive $i$, the quantity that replaces opacity in the volumetric model. Uppercase
$\Sigma_i$ is its \emph{covariance matrix}, and uppercase $\Sigma$ without a subscript is
an ordinary summation. Standing alone, as in a ``$3\sigma$ cut-off'', $\sigma$ is a \emph{standard deviation}
and has nothing to do with density. Where a formula uses more than one of these, the surrounding text says which.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{ll}
\toprule
\textbf{Symbol} & \textbf{Meaning}\\
\midrule
$c_i,\ \Sigma_i$ & Centre and covariance of Gaussian primitive $i$\\
$R_i,\ S_i$ & Its rotation and scale factors, $\Sigma_i = R_i S_i S_i^{\!\top} R_i^{\!\top}$\\
$\alpha_i$ & Opacity of primitive $i$, photometric model only, $\alpha_i \in [0,1]$\\
$\sigma_i$ & Density of primitive $i$, volumetric model only, $\sigma_i \ge 0$\\
$\mu(x)$ & Linear attenuation coefficient at $x$\\
$\tau(r)$ & Line integral of $\mu$ along ray $r$; $\tau_{\max}\approx 9.21$ is its
expressible ceiling\\
$k,\ k_\ell$ & Global and per-layer calibration scalars applied to $\sigma$\\
$T_{ij}$ & Rigid transform from frame $i$ to frame $j$, $T_{ij} \in SE(3)$\\
$\varepsilon_{\mathrm{RMS}}$ & Root-mean-square residual of a registration, in mm\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\subsection{The primitive}

A scene is a set of $N$ anisotropic Gaussians. Primitive $i$ has a centre
$c_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$ and a covariance factorised into rotation and scale so that it stays
positive semi-definite under gradient descent,

\[
  \Sigma_i \;=\; R_i S_i S_i^{\!\top} R_i^{\!\top},
  \qquad R_i \in SO(3), \quad S_i = \operatorname{diag}(s_{i1}, s_{i2}, s_{i3}),
\]

with $R_i$ parameterised by a unit quaternion and $s_{ij} > 0$. Its influence at a point is

\[
  G(x \mid c_i, \Sigma_i) \;=\;
  \exp\!\Big( -\tfrac{1}{2} (x - c_i)^{\!\top} \Sigma_i^{-1} (x - c_i) \Big).
\]

For interchange, a primitive is treated as compactly supported on the ellipsoid
$(x-c_i)^\top \Sigma_i^{-1}(x-c_i) \le 9$, the Mahalanobis-radius-3 cut-off that the Khronos
extension assumes \cite{khrgs}. This is the ``$3\sigma$'' of the standard-deviation sense
above, not a density. The rasteriser used in the fitting experiments truncates at up to 3.33
standard deviations, less for low-opacity primitives.

\subsection{Two different physics on the same primitive}

\paragraph{Photometric splatting: appearance,} the original formulation \cite{kerbl2023}.
Each primitive carries an opacity $\alpha_i \in [0,1]$ and a view-dependent colour in
spherical harmonics. Pixels are formed by front-to-back alpha compositing over the
depth-sorted primitives covering them,

\[
  C \;=\; \sum_{i=1}^{N} c_i \, \alpha_i \prod_{j<i} \big(1 - \alpha_j\big),
\]

which is \emph{order-dependent}: the same set of primitives composited in a different order
gives a different pixel. The diffuse colour is recovered from the zeroth harmonic through
the basis constant $Y_0 = 1/(2\sqrt{\pi}) \approx 0.2820947918$ and the training bias,

\[
  \mathrm{RGB} \;=\; Y_0 \cdot \mathrm{SH}_{0,0} \;+\; \tfrac{1}{2}.
\]

\paragraph{Volumetric splatting: attenuation,} the model used for X-ray view synthesis
and tomographic reconstruction \cite{xgaussian,r2gaussian}. X-ray attenuation
is isotropic and unbounded, so opacity is replaced by a density $\sigma_i \ge 0$ and the
harmonics are discarded entirely. The field models the linear attenuation coefficient

\[
  \mu(x) \;=\; \sum_{i=1}^{N} \sigma_i \, G(x \mid c_i, \Sigma_i),
\]

and a detector value follows the Beer--Lambert law along the ray $r$,

\[
  \tau(r) \;=\; \int_r \mu(x)\, \mathrm{d}s,
  \qquad
  I \;=\; I_0 \, e^{-\tau(r)} .
\]

Because $\tau$ is an integral, this composition is \textbf{order-independent}: the same
primitives in any order give the same value, which is a materially different object from
alpha compositing, and it is why the container refuses to let the two share a vocabulary
without a descriptor saying which is which \cite{uosspec}.

\subsection{Renderer thresholds and numerical calibration}\label{app:ceiling}

The volumetric experiments fit the density field with \code{gsplat}, a rasteriser built
for the photometric model. The field is expressed in $\sigma$, but the rasteriser's
numerics are defined on $\alpha$. The source inspected is release 1.5.3; the version used in
the experiments was not recorded. At a pixel, a primitive whose alpha is below
$\alpha_{\min} = 1/255$ is skipped; a primitive below that threshold everywhere contributes
nothing and receives no gradient. Each primitive's alpha is clamped at 0.999, and
compositing stops before a pixel's transmittance falls to $10^{-4}$. Accumulated alpha
therefore stays below 0.9999, and through $\tau = -\ln(1 - \alpha_{\mathrm{acc}})$ the
expressible line integral is bounded by
\[
  \tau_{\max} \;=\; \ln 10^{4} \;\approx\; 9.21 .
\]
The opacity threshold and this ceiling are different quantities, not the bounds of one
physical interval, and both are implementation choices. In the reported experiment,
unscaled densities gave about 19\,dB projection PSNR before calibration.

\paragraph{What calibration establishes.} A positive global scalar rescales the field,
$\sigma_i'=k\sigma_i$, so that the mean rendered projection matches the mean of the
reference digitally reconstructed radiograph. This changes the density
scale without directly moving Gaussian centres or covariances. It is a numerical fitting
step; it does not calibrate a CBCT scanner to Hounsfield units or prove preservation of
all anatomical detail after optimisation. The reported projection score rose to about
43\,dB. The result concerns that rendering and fitting pipeline.

\subsection{Additive decomposition into layers}

Because $\tau$ is a line integral, it is additive over any partition of the field. If
$\{P_1, \dots, P_L\}$ partitions the primitives disjointly and exhaustively, then

\[
  \tau(r) \;=\; \sum_{\ell=1}^{L} \tau_\ell(r),
  \qquad
  \tau_\ell(r) \;=\; \int_r \sum_{i \in P_\ell} \sigma_i\, G(x \mid c_i, \Sigma_i) \,\mathrm{d}s ,
\]

so the unscaled partition preserves the line integral. Independently fitted or scaled
layers require a separate reconstruction check; additivity of the partition alone does
not guarantee their agreement with the source. We verified this before
comparing anything, measuring a maximum additivity error of $3.6 \times 10^{-7}$ on
normalised $\tau$, which allows a single field and a five-layer decomposition to be scored
\emph{on the same image}.


Each fitted layer receives a separate numerical calibration. This changes the optimisation
and culling behaviour, and the reported runs retain substantially more active primitives.
A controlled ablation would be needed to separate calibration, tissue partitioning and
final model capacity as causes of the improved scores.

\subsection{Appearance sampled onto a surface}

Recovering a per-vertex colour from a fitted appearance field is a weighted mean over
nearby primitives; it is a surface-colour estimator, not the view-dependent compositing
equation above. For the $k = 32$ nearest primitives within a search radius of three times
the 99th-percentile primitive scale,
\[
  w_i(v) \;=\; \alpha_i \exp\!\Big(-\frac{\lVert v-c_i\rVert^2}{2\,\bar s_i^{\,2}}\Big),
  \qquad
  \mathrm{RGB}(v) \;=\; \frac{\sum_i w_i(v)\, \mathrm{RGB}_i}{\sum_i w_i(v)} ,
\]
where $\bar s_i$ is the mean of the three scale factors of primitive $i$, an isotropic
approximation to $G(v\mid c_i,\Sigma_i)$. A vertex is marked measured only if
$\sum_i w_i(v) > 10^{-6}$ and at least one neighbour lies within $3\bar s_i$; otherwise it
receives a declared neutral colour. A display-only fill from measured neighbours of the same
tooth does not change that flag. The expression has no visibility order or transmittance,
so it is not the colour a camera necessarily sees at that vertex.

\subsection{Thresholding the crown boundary}\label{app:otsu}

Otsu's method selects the threshold $t^\star$ on the CIELAB $a^\star$ channel that maximises
the between-class variance,

\[
  t^\star = \arg\max_t \; \omega_0(t)\,\omega_1(t)\,\big(\mu_0(t) - \mu_1(t)\big)^2 ,
\]

and the reported separation is the standardised distance between the resulting class
means, $d = |\mu_0 - \mu_1| / \sqrt{(\sigma_0^2 + \sigma_1^2)/2}$, where $\sigma_0$ and
$\sigma_1$ are within-class standard deviations, not densities. Values of 3.4--4.3 were
obtained on four photographs of case~A. Since the classes are the ones Otsu chose, $d$
measures bimodality of the histogram, not agreement with anatomy.

\subsection{Reporting metric}

Reconstruction quality is peak signal-to-noise ratio on held-out views,
$\mathrm{PSNR} = 10 \log_{10} (M^2 / \mathrm{MSE})$ with $M$ the dynamic range. For the reported volumetric comparison, 252 projection views per case were generated
and one in eight was retained for evaluation. Comparisons use the same target image.
The appearance scores in \S\ref{sec:negative} instead refer to training views. Projection
PSNR does not measure error on native-resolution slices or performance on a diagnostic task.
